🇰🇭 CSB Vaults

ERC-4626 over KHRt — one plain, one gated, side by side

What this is

Two vaults holding the same asset, deployed together, differing by one function. The plain one is OpenZeppelin's ERC-4626 with nothing added. The compliant one adds a single hook that checks identity before a share moves. Everything else — the maths, the interface, the rounding — is identical.

That makes this a controlled comparison rather than a demonstration. Run the same action against both and the only thing that can explain a difference in outcome is the hook.

The test that matters

ERC-4626's deposit takes a receiver. You pay the assets; somebody else gets the shares. Point it at an address with no KYC attestation and see which vault allows it.

Simulated with eth_call — it sends nothing and costs nothing. It reports what each vault would do.

What the experiment found

1. The plain vault hands exposure over in a single call. No transfer of the share is needed and no second step is involved: an attested holder deposits KHRt and names an unattested receiver, who ends up holding a claim on pooled regulated money. This is the sharpest version of the finding, because the escape is not a loophole in the protocol — it is a documented parameter of the standard, present in every conforming implementation.
2. Redemption still fails. The unattested holder cannot pull KHRt out, because the vault's transfer of the underlying hits the perimeter. So no riel escapes; what escapes is the economic exposure to it, exactly as with Uniswap LP tokens and Aave's aTokens.
3. One hook closes it. The compliant vault gates the share itself, so the same call reverts before anything is issued. The cost is that the vault is no longer a standard ERC-4626 — a composing protocol that assumed shares move freely will break against it, and that trade is the whole architectural question in one function.

The perimeter governs custody; composability governs exposure. Three protocols now say the same thing, by three different mechanisms.
