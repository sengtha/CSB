Built for the threats that are coming, not the ones that already happened

Two failure modes will define the next decade of financial infrastructure: AI-driven attacks that make credential theft and social engineering cheap at scale, and quantum computers that eventually break the signatures every blockchain relies on. A sovereign, permissioned chain can answer both in ways a public chain and a traditional bank structurally cannot.

🛡 Against AI-driven attack — multisig everywhere

No single compromised human, key, or machine is enough.
The realistic AI attack is not breaking cryptography. It is convincingly impersonating an official, at scale, until someone approves something.
  • No unilateral authority. The validator manager, every precompile admin address, and every administrative contract role are held by institutional multisigs — no official below the council can act alone.
  • Powers are split across institutions. Identity issuance, asset freezing, and money issuance are three different authorities. Compromising one gets you one power, not the chain.
  • Every enforcement action carries a court-order reference on chain. An action without one is not just wrong, it is impossible.
  • Validators sign blocks with HSM-protected node keys — no human approves individual blocks, so there is no per-block human to deceive.
  • A stolen key is not a lost account. Identity-bound recovery reissues to a new address under the same registered identity — the attacker's advantage expires.
  • Tamper-evident across institutions. Altering the record requires collusion between separately-run validators, and leaves evidence with all of them.
Stated honestly: this is resilience, not immunity. It does not stop an attack; it makes a single successful compromise insufficient to move money, and makes the attempt visible to parties the attacker does not control.

🔐 Against quantum — crypto-agility by governance

The ability to change cryptography is worth more than any single algorithm.
Every EVM chain depends on ECDSA. A cryptographically relevant quantum computer breaks it — and on a public ledger, the keys it will break are already published, waiting.
  • Upgradeable signature verification. Accounts are smart accounts whose signing scheme can change: ECDSA today, ML-DSA / Falcon-class when the standards and tooling mature — keeping the same address, with no asset migration.
  • A governed validator set can actually execute the upgrade. Coordinated, fleet-wide cryptographic migration is a decision here. On a public chain it is a years-long social negotiation that may simply fail.
  • "Private to the world" limits harvest-now-decrypt-later. Public ledgers expose every public key permanently, so an adversary can collect today and break later. CSB's ledger is not world-readable, so there is far less to harvest in the first place.
  • Migration is a planned phase, not an emergency response written after the news breaks.
Stated honestly: post-quantum signatures are not deployed today — the tooling is not ready anywhere. What exists now is the architecture that makes switching possible without asking every citizen to move their money, plus the governance able to decide it. That is quantum readiness, not quantum proof, and anyone claiming the latter today should be read carefully.

The rest, briefly: Avalanche L1 (Subnet-EVM), permissioned proof-of-authority — public institutions as validators (placeholder) · Identity-Authority-issued on-chain KYC with no personal data on chain, one address per identity by default · tokenized riel with a pluggable issuer · gas priced at about 1 riel per payment and routed to a public-good fund rather than burned · a single governed egress gateway wrapping Avalanche ICTT with a council-controlled token allowlist, minimum tiers, daily caps and a circuit breaker.
